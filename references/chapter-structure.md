# Chapter structure

A chapter is a change-bearing unit, not a fixed word-count shell. Its blueprint
declares the scene sequence and its Settlement records the resulting state.

## Scene contract

Every scene names:

- a concrete immediate goal;
- the viewpoint and characters present;
- a pressure, conflict, or unanswered question;
- an observable action and information change;
- the exit state that hands off to the next scene.

## Rhythm

Use a readable opening, an expanding middle, and a consequential ending. The
proportions are project choices. A quiet scene is valid when it changes a
relationship, reveals information, places a constraint, or prepares a later
choice. Repetition without change is a warning, not a genre rule.

## Hooks

Hooks are explicit records with an ID and lifecycle (`introduced`, `progressed`,
`paid-off`, `retired`). A chapter may advance only the hooks declared in its
blueprint. The ending should leave a next action, question, relationship shift,
or consequence that the next blueprint can name.
